Quick overview

How a small data-loader change improves over filtering or co-training on imperfect robot data.

01 Clean data is scarce.

Expert target demos are expensive. Easier data is messier: failures, simulation, other tasks, and other robots.

02 Messy data is partly right.

A rollout may share the plan but have bad control, or share local skills while solving the wrong task.

03 Noise creates overlap.

At high enough noise, target and imperfect actions can look the same. Low noise reveals local motion details.

04 Ambient labels useful windows.

A sweep or classifier marks the noise levels where each extra source matches the target behavior.

05 The sampler enforces them.

Choose the noise level first. Target data is always valid; extra data is used only in its allowed window.

Detailed walkthrough

1. Start With Robot Data

The clean imitation-learning story starts with expert demonstrations from the exact robot, task, and environment you care about. In practice, that data is the expensive part. What you can collect or reuse is usually a patchwork: non-expert teleoperation, simulation, other tasks, other robots, and large mixed datasets such as Open X-Embodiment.

The extra data is not all good or all bad. A non-expert rollout may take the right route with clumsy motion. A pick-and-place dataset may teach reliable grasps while encoding the wrong bin choice. Filtering is too blunt; co-training is too trusting. Ambient asks a narrower question: at which diffusion noise levels does this data match the target behavior?

2. Use the Noise Level as a Filter

Ambient keeps Diffusion Policy mostly untouched. The policy still learns a denoiser for noisy action chunks. The key question is what the denoiser can still see at each noise level.

High-noise intuition

High noise can make suboptimal and target data look the same.

At low noise, a denoiser can see the exact mistakes: jagged motions, bad contacts, or clumsy teleoperation. After adding enough Gaussian noise, those low-level differences are hidden. What remains is the shared coarse behavior, such as the route through a maze or the object that should be manipulated.

That explains the high-noise window. If target and extra actions overlap after noising, extra data can train those denoisers. The low-noise window below is a separate case: it relies on locality, not full-distribution overlap.

Low noise

The full target and extra distributions are visibly different.

High noise

High noise: contraction

Enough Gaussian noise washes out contact-level mistakes. If imperfect data shares the same coarse intent as target data, the noisy versions can overlap and teach high-level behavior.

Low noise: locality

Low noise is not about the full datasets looking the same. It is where robot denoisers act locally, so Ambient can reuse transferable motions such as grasping or placing even when the broader task is different.

In between: be conservative

Some noise levels still expose the wrong dynamics, wrong task logic, or poor control. Ambient leaves those denoisers to the target data.

Same policy. Same inference. More selective training batches.

3. What You Actually Implement

The method has two short phases: label when non-target data is allowed, then make the data loader respect those labels. The trained policy is still a standard Diffusion Policy.

Phase 1

Label the extra data

Split demonstrations into target data Dp and extra data Dq. A hyperparameter sweep is often enough. The paper also gives a classifier option: train cφ(At, t) to tell whether a noisy action came from Dp or Dq.

Classifier

Find where distributions blur together

tmin is the first noise level where noisy Dq actions look like noisy target actions. Practically, it is where the classifier can no longer separate the two.

Optional

Mark local-transfer windows

tmax marks low-noise timesteps where local motion primitives transfer. It can be estimated with a similar classifier, or chosen with a task-specific sweep.

Data-loader change

  • Store allowed timestep windows for each extra data source.
  • Sample the diffusion time t before choosing the data source.
  • Always allow target data Dp; allow extra data only when its window contains t.

Everything else stays standard

  • The Diffusion Policy architecture.
  • The usual denoising objective used in the experiments.
  • The deployment-time denoising process and runtime policy.
  • The same idea also applies to flow-matching policies.

4. Results

The paper first builds controlled cases where we know which part of the extra data should transfer. Then it tests the same sampler with Open X-Embodiment data on real Kuka tasks.

Controlled checks

Each task makes the partial match between target and extra data visible.

Noisy trajectories

Keep the routes, lose the jerkiness.

In the 2D maze, RRT gives broad coverage but produces jagged paths. Ambient reaches 99.5% success and lowers the smoothness metric from 62.2 to 31.0, where lower is smoother.

Sim-to-real proxy

Take the simulated strategy, not the sim2real gap.

In planar pushing, per-datapoint t_min annotation reaches 93.5% success, compared with 84.5% for co-training.

Task mismatch

Borrow the grasp, not the wrong bin choice.

In block sorting, opposite-task data has useful pick-and-place motion but the wrong task logic. Ambient reaches 93.3% success versus 22.7% for co-training.

Real hardware scaling

The OXE experiments test whether Ambient can use a broader usable robot mixture instead of filtering back to a curated subset.

Open X-Embodiment scaling

Using more OXE beats filtering to the curated mix.

Magic Soup++ is the curated 27-dataset OXE mixture used by OpenVLA. Custom OXE is not more selective; it is our broad usable OXE subset for this setup: Magic Soup++ plus 21 more compatible datasets. The OXE datasets left out were mostly inaccessible, bimanual, missing frequency metadata, or blocked by unresolved loader issues. Co-training plateaus as the mixture grows; Ambient improves when the extra OXE data is added.

That is the scaling result: data that would normally be filtered can still help when it trains only the diffusion timesteps where it matches the target task. On real Kuka tasks, Ambient improves table cleaning by up to 15% and builds towers up to 33% taller than co-training.

Stop choosing between filtering and blanket co-training.

If extra robot data is partly useful, Ambient is the safer default: keep the data, but train from it only at the diffusion timesteps where it matches the target behavior. In these experiments, that tiny data-loader change consistently improves over throwing data away or co-training on the whole mixture.

Common questions

Questions & Answers

Does the classifier need to be perfect?

No. In planar pushing, performance was not very sensitive to the classifier checkpoint or threshold. The classifier just needs to be good enough to find the noise levels where extra data starts to look like target data.

When should low-noise extra data help?

When the local motion is transferable. It clearly helped in task mismatch, where pick-and-place motion transferred but task logic did not. It also appears to help in tower building. It did not help when low-level contacts changed, as in sim-to-real pushing.

Do I still need careful dataset weights?

Less than with co-training. Ambient was at most 9% worse without the OXE re-weighting scheme, while unweighted co-trained policies were too unsafe to run on hardware. Timestep selection makes the mixture less brittle.

Why not just fine-tune after co-training?

Fine-tuning did not close the gap. Fine-tuning an Ambient base policy beat fine-tuning a co-trained base policy, and with scarce target data, the Ambient base could already beat the fine-tuned co-trained one. On OXE table cleaning, fine-tuning did not produce a statistically significant gain.

Does this solve observation shift too?

Not directly. Ambient is aimed at action-space shift. The paper also tried noising observations and classifier-free guidance on table cleaning, but those additions did not improve the policy in that setting.

Is there a downside?

The main cost is training compute. Ambient is comparable to ordinary co-training because both keep the extra data, but it is more expensive than data filtering because filtering throws that data away. In our experiments, the extra cost was usually worth it.

Abstract

We propose Ambient Diffusion Policy, a simple and principled method for imitation learning from suboptimal data in robotics. High-quality, task-specific robot data is expensive and time-consuming to collect, while suboptimal datasets with lower-quality or out-of-distribution demonstrations are abundant. Existing methods that co-train on both data sources in robotics often fail to separate the meaningful and the harmful features in suboptimal samples. In contrast, our method attempts to extract only the useful learning signal by introducing a new axis to co-training in robotics: noise-dependent data usage.

Ambient Diffusion Policy restricts the contribution of suboptimal data during training to only the high and low diffusion times. We observe that robot action data exhibits a spectral power law, which induces two important properties on the optimal Diffusion Policy: locality and a global-to-local hierarchy. These properties rigorously justify our algorithm.

Our experiments validate Ambient Diffusion Policy on four types of suboptimal action data (noisy trajectories, sim-to-real gap, task mismatch, and large-scale data mixtures) and six tasks. The results show that it effectively learns from arbitrary sources of suboptimal data. Notably, it outperforms existing baselines by up to 33% when scaled to Open X-Embodiment—a large real-world dataset with unstructured and heterogeneous distribution shifts. Overall, Ambient Diffusion Policy increases the utility of suboptimal demonstrations and expands the set of usable data sources in robotics.
